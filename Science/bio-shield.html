Phase 1 of 3 · Non-Specific Barrier Defense

The First Line: Skin & Lysozymes

The body's first defense is physical and chemical. Intact skin — tightly-packed epithelial cells — blocks pathogen entry. Lysozymes in tears, saliva, and mucus destroy bacteria by breaking peptidoglycan bonds in their cell walls. Most pathogens never progress further.

📖 A-Level CheckpointSkin provides non-specific defense — it blocks ALL pathogen types equally, with no memory. Lysozymes are enzymes targeting bacterial cell wall structure. This is the first line of defense: always active, non-adaptive.
Phase 2 of 3 · Innate Immune Response

Phagocytosis & APC Formation

Macrophages detect chemical distress signals and move toward the threat (chemotaxis). They engulf the pathogen into a phagosome, digest it, then display fragments on MHC Class II proteins — becoming an Antigen-Presenting Cell (APC).

📖 The Critical BridgeThe APC displayed antigen activates T-Helper Cells → which release cytokines → which activate B-Cells → which produce antibodies. Without phagocytosis and APC formation, the adaptive response cannot begin.
